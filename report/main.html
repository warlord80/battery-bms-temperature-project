Abstract

Accurate state-of-charge (SOC) estimation in lithium-ion battery management systems (BMS) is essential for operational safety, cycle life preservation, and energy utilization. However, elevated ambient operating temperatures and progressive capacity/resistance degradation introduce non-negligible model parametric uncertainties and sensor drift that degrade open-loop Coulomb counting. This work develops a reduced-order electrothermal and aging-aware equivalent-circuit model for controlled sensitivity analysis of battery SOC estimation. It demonstrates behaviour under stated assumptions; it does not identify parameters from new experiments or establish a chemistry-independent degradation law.

A first-order (1-RC) Thévenin equivalent circuit model (ECM) is coupled to a lumped single-node thermal differential equation with Arrhenius-governed resistance dynamics and empirical capacity fade surrogates parameterized for a commercial Panasonic NCR18650B (3.2 Ah) NCA/Graphite cell. Across a fully factorial 3×3×2×2=363 \times 3 \times 2 \times 2 = 363×3×2×2=36-scenario matrix spanning ambient temperatures (25 ∘C,35 ∘C,45 ∘C25\,^\circ\text{C}, 35\,^\circ\text{C}, 45\,^\circ\text{C}25∘C,35∘C,45∘C), state-of-health levels (SOH=1.00,0.90,0.80\text{SOH} = 1.00, 0.90, 0.80SOH=1.00,0.90,0.80), and load profiles (steady discharge vs. dynamic pulse cycles), an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) observer is evaluated against nominal Coulomb counting.

Results demonstrate that while Coulomb counting accumulates severe root-mean-square errors (RMSE up to 15.01%15.01\%15.01%) due to unmodeled capacity loss, sensor bias, and initial state offsets, the physics-informed EKF maintains root-mean-square errors below 0.89%0.89\%0.89% across all scenarios, achieving an average RMSE of 0.38%0.38\%0.38% and converging within 5 seconds under a −15%-15\%−15% initial state offset. Tornado sensitivity analysis reveals that initial state uncertainty (±20%\pm 20\%±20%) and capacity fade (SOH=0.80\text{SOH} = 0.80SOH=0.80) dominate estimation drift, whereas ambient thermal elevation primarily modulates internal ohmic dissipation (28.1 ∘C28.1\,^\circ\text{C}28.1∘C to 47.2 ∘C47.2\,^\circ\text{C}47.2∘C). Limitations inherent to lumped single-node approximations and empirical degradation laws are discussed alongside prospective laboratory validation protocols.

1. Introduction

Lithium-ion energy storage systems represent the foundation of modern electrified transportation and stationary grid storage. Within battery management systems (BMS), real-time estimation of internal states—predominantly the State of Charge (SOC, z∈[0,1]z \in [0, 1]z∈[0,1]) and State of Health (SOH)—is vital for state prediction, thermal runaway prevention, and charging regulation (Plett 2004; Mehta & Gupta 2024). Unlike voltage and temperature, SOC cannot be measured directly with physical sensors; it must be inferred from observable terminal metrics, namely terminal voltage (VtV_tVt​), applied current (III), and surface temperature (TTT).

In commercial applications, open-loop current integration (Coulomb counting) serves as the traditional baseline due to its computational simplicity. However, Coulomb counting suffers from fundamental vulnerabilities:

  1. Susceptibility to unmodeled initial state offsets (z^0≠z0\hat{z}_0 \neq z_0z^0​=z0​);
  2. Systematic drift driven by current sensor measurement bias and precision noise;
  3. Divergence when true cell capacity fades due to cycling or aging (Qmax⁡<QnomQ_{\max} < Q_{\text{nom}}Qmax​<Qnom​), while the estimator relies on the factory nameplate rating (QnomQ_{\text{nom}}Qnom​).

These challenges are exacerbated in harsh environmental operating conditions, particularly elevated ambient temperatures (35 ∘C35\,^\circ\text{C}35∘C to 45 ∘C45\,^\circ\text{C}45∘C). Thermal excitation enhances solid-state diffusion and reaction kinetics (reducing internal resistance via Arrhenius relationships), but simultaneously accelerates chemical degradation mechanisms such as solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) growth, transition metal dissolution, and irreversible lithium inventory loss (Mattia et al. 2025; Sun et al. 2026).

1.1 Research Questions

This computational study is designed to answer four core scientific questions within a disclosed reduced-order model:

  1. Voltage Response: How does the modeled terminal voltage response change with ambient temperature under identical load profiles?
  2. Coulomb Counting Error Accumulation: How much SOC error does nominal-capacity Coulomb counting accumulate when true capacity degrades?
  3. Observer Error Reduction: Does an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) observer robustly eliminate error relative to Coulomb counting under sensor noise, systematic current bias, and initial state offsets?
  4. Dominant Physical Factor: Within this model, which factor exerts the largest effect on estimation fidelity: ambient temperature, load profile dynamics, capacity fade, or internal resistance growth?

2. Related Work and Theoretical Background

Electrochemical state estimation spans a hierarchy of model complexities. Full electrochemical physics models, such as the Doyle–Fuller–Newman (DFN) and pseudo-two-dimensional (P2D) partial differential equation frameworks, capture concentration gradients, pore-scale transport, and Butler–Volmer reaction kinetics (Sulzer et al. 2021). While scientifically rigorous, P2D models demand dozens of microstructural parameters that are difficult to identify non-destructively and entail prohibitive computational overhead for embedded real-time microcontrollers.

Conversely, equivalent-circuit models (ECMs) represent electrochemical behavior via lumped electrical networks (Sun et al. 2026; Barletta et al. 2022). The first-order Thévenin ECM utilizes an open-circuit voltage source, an ohmic resistance R0R_0R0​, and a single parallel resistor-capacitor (R1∥C1R_1 \parallel C_1R1​∥C1​) branch representing activation and concentration polarization. Barletta et al. (2022) demonstrated that parameterizing a 1-RC Thévenin model against experimental pulse discharge data on commercial Panasonic NCR18650B cylindrical cells yields terminal voltage root-mean-square errors below 20 mV20\,\text{mV}20mV, validating its balance of physical expressiveness and computational speed.

Thermal coupling is critical: internal cell temperature governs both instantaneous overpotentials and degradation rates. Mehta & Gupta (2024) and Mattia et al. (2025) reviewed electrothermal coupling methodologies, highlighting that lumped zero-dimensional thermal models accurately track core temperatures in cylindrical 18650 cells under moderate CCC-rates (≤1C\le 1C≤1C), where Biot numbers remain sufficiently small (Bi<0.1\text{Bi} < 0.1Bi<0.1) to justify negligible internal spatial thermal gradients.

For closed-loop state estimation, Kalman filtering frameworks (specifically Extended Kalman Filters and Unscented Kalman Filters) have emerged as the industry standard (Plett 2004; Sun et al. 2026). By incorporating terminal voltage measurement feedback through analytical linearization (Jacobians) and stochastic error covariance tracking, the EKF enables asymptotic convergence even in the presence of corrupted initial conditions.

6.2 Influence of Elevated Temperature

Elevated temperature exerts two opposing effects:

  1. Short-Term Electrical Advantage: Arrhenius kinetics accelerate charge transfer, reducing R0R_0R0​ and R1R_1R1​. Consequently, overpotential uncertainties contribute less error to terminal voltage predictions, yielding superior EKF tracking (RMSE drops from 0.34%0.34\%0.34% at 25 ∘C25\,^\circ\text{C}25∘C to 0.17%0.17\%0.17% at 45 ∘C45\,^\circ\text{C}45∘C).

7. Limitations and Future Work

To maintain strict scientific integrity, the following limitations of this study must be disclosed:

  1. Lumped Thermal Model: The single-node thermal ODE assumes a spatially uniform core temperature. While valid for single 18650 cells at ≤1C\le 1C≤1C under natural convection (Bi<0.1\text{Bi} < 0.1Bi<0.1), it neglects radial thermal gradients present under fast charging (≥3C\ge 3C≥3C) or in dense battery modules.
  2. Empirical Aging Surrogate: The capacity fade and resistance growth models are reduced-order empirical power-law relations rather than microstructural models of SEI layer growth, active material cracking, or lithium plating.
  3. No Direct Laboratory Measurements: All results presented herein are reproducible computational simulations based on literature parameters (Barletta et al. 2022). They do not constitute new physical laboratory experiments.
